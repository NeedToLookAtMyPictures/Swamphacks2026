How a trail report spreads without cell service

A northbound hiker passes a dry spring at km 72 and posts it. There's no signal, and most hikers are behind them, walking the same way, so the report has to travel against the flow: carried by the few southbound hikers, or by relay boxes at the shelters. Does it reach the hikers walking toward the spring before they get there? Same hikers, same days, with and without relay boxes.

How this is modelled

  • Trail: 100 km with a shelter every 10 km. Most hikers go north (85% by default; on the AT the large majority of thru-hikers do). Each walks 2.8–4.2 km/h from around 7:00, heads for the next shelter after 17:30 and camps there (or wherever they are at 20:00). Hikers leaving the section are replaced by new hikers who haven't heard anything.
  • Radio: each minute, a device learns the news from any device within LoRa range that already had it, so news moves at most one hop per minute (devices wake every 20 s and a small LoRa exchange took about 1.5 s on the Heltec boards). The range is a setting. 2 km is a cautious figure for SF7 in forest; it hasn't been measured in the field yet.
  • Relay boxes: a solar board at each shelter that's always on and stores and passes on whatever it hears. Its antenna is mounted up high, so it also reaches the boxes at the neighbouring shelters (10 km). That's an assumption, realistic for elevated LoRa antennas but not yet tested, and it lets news move along the trail in both directions whichever way people walk.
  • "Warned": a hiker who reaches km 72 after the report was made, and already knew when they got there.
  • This is an encounter-level model built for scale. The protocol itself, packet by packet, is tested by trailmesh/sim.py and on the real boards.
